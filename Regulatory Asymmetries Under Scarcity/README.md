# Regulatory Asymmetries Under Scarcity in an Instrumented Cognitive System

## Paper Overview

This paper demonstrates asymmetric regulatory responses in cognitive systems under resource scarcity, where adaptive regulation maintains epistemic restraint while coercive approaches destabilize, validating learned intervention intelligence in the Regulatory Intelligence paradigm.

## Theoretical Foundation

This work validates the Regulatory Intelligence (RI) paradigm from "The Regulatory Intelligence Paradigm: Geometric Homeostasis and Elastic Cognition in Synthetic Neurosymbolic Organisms" \cite{Cragin2026Thesis}, demonstrating that geometric homeostasis enables learned intervention intelligence under scarcity.

Key RI concepts validated:
- Intervention intelligence: beneficial regulation without iatrogenic harm
- Elastic adaptation vs. brittle collapse under stress
- Epistemic restraint: learning when not to intervene
- Clean-slate falsification ensuring no persistent learning
- Four-lobe architecture enabling adaptive regulatory responses

## Key Findings

- Under identical scarcity regimes, adaptive regulation shows φ_max ≈ observer baseline (30.10° vs 31.99°)
- Coercive regulation overshoots significantly (47.75°)
- Statistical significance: p=0.0008 between observer and regulator conditions
- Demonstrates learned intervention intelligence under pressure
